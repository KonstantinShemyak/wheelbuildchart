Wheel Tension Chart: what if my spoke is not supported?

WheelTensionChart shows more spokes than tensometer vendors do

The spoke dropdown lists start with spoke types listed in the tables provided by the tensometer vendor.

Then, a separator --- estimated --- follows.

Below the separator, WheelTensionChart lists spokes which are not listed by the vendor, but which it can reliably estimate.

How the estimation is done

Suppose your tensometer does not list your spoke, but some other tensometer does; call the latter donor. If both tensometers list some spoke similar to yours - call it reference spoke - the estimation is done like this:

  1. From your tensometer's table, your reading is taken as the tension of the reference spoke.
  2. From the donor tensometer's table, that tension gives what the donor tensometer would read on the reference spoke.
  3. That reading is taken as the estimation of tension of your spoke.

Example: you have spokes 1.8mm, but your tensometer MyTenso does not list them. Fortunately, WheelTensionChart knows another tensometer, ExpensiveTenso, which lists spokes 1.8mm, and spoke 2mm is listed in both MyTenso and ExpensiveTenso.

If SpokeTensionChart can determine that MyTenso and ExpensiveTenso behave similarly, and spokes 1.8mm and 2mm are "close enough", then ExpensiveTenso can be used as donor tensometer and 2mm as the reference spoke.

  1. You measure your spoke; let's say it gives reading 20.
  2. SpokeTensionChart finds reading 20 in the table for ExpensiveTenso, for 2mm spoke.
  3. Let's say it is produced by tension 80kgf.
  4. SpokeTensionChart looks in the table for ExpensiveTenso which reading would show 80kgf for 1.8mm spoke. Let's say it's 100kgf. This is shown as estimated tension for 1.8mm spoke by MyTenso.

What is "similarly behaving tensometers" and "close enough spokes"?

Tensometers

When looking for a donor for your tensometer, SpokeTensionChart checks the spokes both of them list. It pretends that each such spoke is missing from your tensometer's table, estimates it, and compares with the provided value. Only a tensometer whose estimates are within 10% on average within range 60-160 kgf is accepted as donor. If there and several, the best one is picked.

Data shows that only tensometer of a similar construction can work well as donor.

Spokes

WheelTensionChart estimates a spoke only through a reference of the same kind (round vs bladed, same material). Within that, it uses a previously chosen donor tensometer only if the data shows that the donor predicts your same-kind spokes within 10%. Cross-kind estimates are excluded because there's not enough data to confirm them, and the few available cases show poor correlation.

Achieved Precision

Across all the spokes which the vendors list, estimating the existing spoke under these rules kept the median error at 5%, and 93% of the errors under 10%.

With a tensometer of construction similar to e.g. ParkTool TM-1, the precision of the reading itself is probably around 2-5%. So, the estimations the tool offers are somewhat less accurate than the "first-hand" data for a home mechanic, but not too much.

Summary

For spokes not listed by the vendor, SpokeTensionChart offers all estimations where the vendor data gives evidence for reliability of such estimation. If the spoke is not in the list, not even under --- estimated --- separator, vendor data does not provide grounds for reliable estimation.
